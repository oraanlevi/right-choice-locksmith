Know the Difference

Rekey or Replace? Here's How to Choose

Rekeying and lock replacement both change who can open your door — but they work differently. The right answer depends on the condition of your hardware and what you're trying to accomplish. See our full decision guide →

🔑

Rekey if your hardware is working fine

Rekeying replaces the internal pins of your existing lock so old keys no longer work, without touching the hardware itself. Rekeying can often be a simpler option when the existing hardware is still in good condition. See our rekeying service →

🔨

Replace if the hardware is damaged or worn

A forced entry attempt, years of heavy use, or a lock that sticks and grinds can leave hardware that isn't reliable. Rekeying a worn or compromised lock gives you new keys for a failing mechanism — replacement addresses the actual problem.

⬆️

Replace if you're upgrading or changing hardware type

Adding a deadbolt where there was only a knob lock, or moving to different hardware entirely, requires full replacement. We remove the existing lock and install the new one.

What to Expect

What Happens During a Lock Change

🔩

The old hardware comes out

We remove the existing lock cylinder and hardware from your door. If the lock is damaged, we extract it carefully to avoid unnecessary marking of the door or frame.

🔧

New hardware goes in

We install the replacement lock and check the fit against the door frame. A lock that doesn't align with the door properly won't operate correctly.

✂️

New keys provided

New keys are provided for the replacement hardware. Before finishing, we confirm the lock operates properly.
